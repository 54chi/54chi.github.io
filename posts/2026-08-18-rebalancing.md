I confess I'm an avid manga/anime consumer. It's a good escape valve, and I get to practice some Japanese. Sometimes I learn other things from them too.

One of those things have been the concept of **"slow life"**: the protagonist is fed up of his/her job and switch into a simpler life (sometimes in another world), like crafting, farming, etc. abandoning the stresses of modern life. 

For years now, I've been pretty grumpy with my life. While the corporate job pays very well, I couldn't help but getting more and more frustrated at the lack of willing to do what needs to be done because they expect someone else to do it. Call it quiet quitting, or call it laziness, I was never good at them. I have been projecting my frustrations and my idea of "fairness" on others,  which -ironically- is very unfair to them.

So, thanks to a recent announcement from the company, I'll be slowing down in life (or so I am proclaiming). Instead of looking for another job immediately, I'll be taking a long long vacation from the corporate world to try to lighten my sour mood, improve the skills that I want to improve and test my idea of "fairness" onto myself. I wonder what I'll see in the mirror a few weeks from now.

Anyways, don't expect me to become a farmer (yet), but when it comes to "crafting", I've always supported the [Software Craftsmanship Manifesto ](https://manifesto.softwarecraftsmanship.org/) , though now that we are surrounded with AI-something everywhere, I plan to check "revised" versions [here](https://ai-manifesto.software-craftsmanship.dev/principles), and [here](https://ericsilva.org/posts/ai-manifesto/), among others before trying to follow them. 

I'll be setting a soft schedule for a few of the projects I have in mind. With the help of AI, YouTube and some luck this may work well.
